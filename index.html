01 / Drawing & stranding
The conductor
Copper, drawn & bunched

8 mm copper rod is drawn through dies and twisted into a conductor — the heart that carries the current.

02 / Insulation
The insulation
PVC / XLPE, extruded

A polymer layer is extruded over the conductor and spark-tested — it stops shock and short circuits.

03 / Inner sheath
Inner sheath (bedding)
Black PVC, extruded

A black PVC bedding is extruded over the laid-up cores — a smooth cushion that beds and protects the armour wires.

04 / Armouring
The armour
Galvanised Steel Wire / Aluminium Wire

Steel wires are wound around the bedding — mechanical protection for burial, impact and pressure.

05 / Sheathing
The sheath
PVC, the outer skin

A final sheath is extruded on — the cable's skin against weather, UV and chemicals. It's complete.

Mechanical protection and earth continuity.

Multi-core · direct-buried · duct

A hidden danger

Sub-Standard CableUndersize Cable

Same outside. Less inside.

An undersized cable carries less copper than the standard requires — yet looks identical from the outside. You can't tell by eye; you only find out when it fails.

Same cable, same outer diameter — only the copper inside is different.

Why is it still around?

01
Cost-cutting Copper is expensive, so some makers quietly use less of it — and the outside diameter still looks the same.
02
Price pressure In a crowded, price-driven market, quality gets compromised to stay the cheapest quote.
03
Low awareness Many buyers don't check the current rating, standard or conductor size — so undersized cable slips through.
04
Weak enforcement Standards exist but aren't strictly policed, so non-compliant cable keeps circulating.

Cheap copper wins because nobody checks — and nobody enforces.

If you choose undersized, here's what it can cause

Mild
Voltage drop Resistance steals voltage at the load — motors run slow, lights dim, sensitive equipment misbehaves.
Damaging
Overheating Too small to carry the full current → higher resistance → more heat → melted insulation.
Costly
Shorter lifespan Constant overheating ages the insulation faster, so the cable fails years earlier than it should.
Worst case
Fire hazard Worst case — the insulation burns through, the conductor is exposed, and a fire can start.

Tonn Cable supplies full-size conductors, built to standard — what's marked on the cable is what's inside.

Updated news

New cable colours, by 2029.

Industry update · Suruhanjaya Tenaga
Directive ST No. 11/2026 · Electricity Supply Act 1990 [Act 447]

The Energy Commission has adopted the IEC 60445 cable colour code for all electrical wiring in Malaysia. From 1 January 2026 the old or new colours may be used — a grace period to switch over.

Mandatory from 1 Jan 2029 Every new, additional or upgraded installation must follow the new colour code.
Old → New · per IEC 60445
  • Phase L1Red→Brown
  • Phase L2Yellow→Black
  • Phase L3Blue→Grey
  • Neutral NBlack→Blue
  • Earth PEGreen-Yellow=Unchanged

Source — Suruhanjaya Tenaga, Arahan ST No. 11/2026. Non-compliance is an offence under s.50E of Act 447.(fine up to RM200,000, up to 2 years' imprisonment, or both).
